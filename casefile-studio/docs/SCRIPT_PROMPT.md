# Writing scripts that feed CaseFile Studio cleanly

Everything below is taken from the running code, not from intent. If a number
changes in `config.py` or `query.py`, change it here too.

## What the app does with your script

You paste raw text. The app slices it into scenes **by character offset** — it
never rewrites a word — then narrates, sources visuals, times captions and
renders. Consequences for how the script must be written:

| Constant | Value | Where | What it means for the writer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narration speed | 150 wpm | `settings.words_per_minute` | 60 min ≈ 9,000 words |
| Scene target | 15 s / **37 words** | `settings.scene_target_sec` | scenes break at sentence ends once ~37 words accumulate |
| Scene cap | 400 | `settings.max_scenes` | beyond this, scenes get merged |
| Caption lines | 4–6 words | `subtitle_service` | breaks at punctuation; long clauses wrap badly |
| Chapters | ~26 scenes ≈ 8 min | `assign_chapters` | plan a beat change every ~1,200 words |
| Ad breaks | every 9 min, 2 min from either end | `suggest_ad_breaks` | leave a natural pause there |

Length targets:

| Video | Words | Scenes | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 min | 2,250 | ~60 | ~3 |
| 30 min | 4,500 | ~120 | ~5 |
| 45 min | 6,750 | ~180 | ~8 |
| 60 min | 9,000 | ~240 | ~10 |

## The two rules that break things if ignored

**1. Every character is narrated.** No headings, no `[SFX]`, no `(pause)`, no
speaker labels, no markdown, no scene numbers, no bracketed stage directions.
The voice will read them aloud. Chapter titles are generated by the app, not
taken from the script.

**2. Sentences are the atom.** The splitter cuts on `. ! ?` followed by
whitespace. Title abbreviations are handled (`Mr Mrs Ms Dr Prof St Jr Sr Lt Sgt
Det Capt Cmdr Gov Sen Rep No vs approx Inc Co` and month abbreviations), so
"Det. Ramirez" stays intact. Unusual abbreviations will split a sentence in the
wrong place. Sentences of 15–35 words work best: shorter and scenes get
choppy, longer and captions wrap awkwardly.

## Concrete nouns steer the B-roll

`query.py` maps ~82 keywords to stock-footage searches. A scene containing one
of these words gets a matching visual; a scene without one falls back to a
generic dark street. **This is the single biggest lever on visual quality.**

Currently recognised (word-boundary matched, longest keyword wins):

> airstrip, aircraft, arrest, bank, bar, boat, body, border, car, cash,
> checkpoint, cocaine, compound, container, convoy, court, courtroom, desert,
> detective, document, drug(s), evidence, extradition, family, funeral, grave,
> gun, handcuffs, harbour/harbor, helicopter, heroin, hotel, indictment,
> informant, jail, jungle, lab/laboratory, laundering, ledger, mansion, market,
> military, money, mountain, murder, narcotics, night, passport, phone, plane,
> police, port, prison, prosecutor, raid, recording, restaurant, runway, safe,
> seizure, shipment, soldier, street, submarine, surveillance, trafficking,
> trial, truck, tunnel, vault, vessel, villa, village, warehouse, winter,
> witness, wiretap

Named people, places and years are extracted separately and sent to **archival**
sources (Internet Archive, Wikimedia) rather than stock. So naming the port, the
city, the agency and the year gives the app something real to find.

## Legal shape of the sentences

The app flags scenes about named real individuals and blocks AI image
generation on them — but it cannot check what you *assert*. For living people
and drug-trafficking allegations this matters more than anywhere else in true
crime:

- State convictions as fact **only where a conviction exists**, and name the
  court and year.
- Everything else is attributed: "prosecutors alleged", "according to the
  indictment", "witnesses testified", "the DEA said at the time".
- Never assert an uncharged person's involvement.
- Acquittals, overturned convictions and pending appeals get said out loud.

## Monetisation shape

YouTube permits crime in documentary context and demonetises gratuitous
detail. Keep violence referenced, not described. No method detail for drug
production. No dwelling on injuries or bodies. The visual vocabulary above is
built the same way — evidence tables and empty rooms, never product or corpses.

# The prompt

Copy everything below into Claude. Fill in the four bracketed slots.

```
You are writing a narration script for a true-crime YouTube documentary channel.

SUBJECT: [e.g. the 1993 arrest of Pablo Escobar, or: the capture of Joaquín
"El Chapo" Guzmán in 2014]
LENGTH: [60] minutes
TONE: measured, factual, documentary. Not sensational, not breathless.
AUDIENCE: adults who want the actual sequence of events and how the case was built.

=== OUTPUT FORMAT — THIS IS STRICT ===

Output ONLY the words to be spoken. Nothing else.

- No headings, no chapter titles, no scene numbers, no timestamps.
- No stage directions, no [SFX], no (pause), no music cues, no speaker labels.
- No markdown, no bullet points, no bold, no asterisks.
- No introduction to me and no sign-off. The first sentence is the first
  sentence of the video.

Every character you write will be read aloud by a text-to-speech voice, so
anything that is not narration will be spoken as if it were.

Separate paragraphs with a blank line. Use paragraphs as beat changes.

=== LENGTH ===

Write approximately [9,000] words. That is [60] minutes at 150 words per
minute. Get within 10% — under-length is a worse failure than over-length.

=== SENTENCE CONSTRUCTION ===

- Sentences of 15 to 35 words. Vary the rhythm; avoid long chains of clauses.
- End sentences with full stops, question marks or exclamation marks only.
- Use standard abbreviations sparingly. "Det." and "Dr." are safe; invented or
  unusual abbreviations are not.
- Spell out anything a voice would stumble over: write "one point five tonnes"
  not "1.5t", "fifteen million dollars" not "$15M", "the D.E.A." as "the DEA".
- Write years in full: "in 1989", not "in '89".

=== HOW TO MAKE THE VISUALS WORK ===

The production tool sources footage by reading each passage for concrete nouns.
Abstract passages get generic filler; concrete ones get matching footage.

In roughly every second or third sentence, ground the narration in a physical
place, object or action. Draw from this vocabulary wherever it is truthful:

airstrip, aircraft, arrest, bank, bar, boat, border, car, cash, checkpoint,
compound, container, convoy, courtroom, desert, detective, document, evidence,
extradition, funeral, handcuffs, harbour, helicopter, hotel, indictment,
informant, jail, jungle, laboratory, ledger, mansion, market, military, money,
mountain, passport, phone, plane, police, port, prison, prosecutor, raid,
recording, restaurant, runway, safe, seizure, shipment, soldier, street,
submarine, surveillance, trafficking, trial, truck, tunnel, vault, vessel,
villa, village, warehouse, winter, witness, wiretap

Name real places, agencies, courts and dates explicitly — "the container port
at Cartagena in March 1991", not "a port in South America". Named places and
years are used to search archival photo libraries, so specificity directly
improves what appears on screen.

=== STRUCTURE ===

Open with 60 to 90 seconds that state plainly what happened and why this case
mattered. Do not tease, do not ask rhetorical questions, do not say "but first".

Then move chronologically. Every 8 minutes or so — roughly every 1,200 words —
change beat: a new phase of the investigation, a new location, a new person.
The tool detects these and turns them into YouTube chapters.

At roughly 9-minute intervals, end a paragraph on a natural pause — a
completed thought, not a cliffhanger. Mid-roll ads are placed there.

Close by stating what happened afterwards: sentences served, appeals, what
changed institutionally, where the surviving figures are now.

=== ACCURACY AND LEGAL CARE — NON-NEGOTIABLE ===

This is about real people, some of them living.

- State a conviction as fact only if the person was actually convicted. Name
  the court and the year.
- Attribute everything else: "prosecutors alleged", "according to the
  indictment", "witnesses testified", "the DEA stated at the time".
- Never state or imply that an uncharged person committed a crime.
- Say plainly when a conviction was overturned, an appeal is pending, or a
  charge was dropped.
- If you are not confident a specific detail is accurate, write around it or
  leave it out. Do not invent quotes, dates, dollar figures, tonnages or names.
- Mark anything you are unsure of by listing it in a separate note AFTER the
  script, under the line "---UNCERTAIN---". Nothing above that line may be
  anything other than narration.

=== WHAT TO KEEP OFF SCREEN ===

The channel needs to stay advertiser-friendly.

- Reference violence; do not describe it. No injury detail, no descriptions of
  bodies, no dwelling on a death.
- No production or synthesis method detail for any drug.
- No glamorising: no admiring descriptions of wealth, and no framing anyone as
  a folk hero.
- Focus on the investigation — the surveillance, the paperwork, the informants,
  the mistakes — rather than the crimes themselves. That is both the safer
  choice and the more interesting story.

Begin the script now.
```

## After Claude writes it

1. Delete anything below `---UNCERTAIN---` before pasting, and check those items.
2. Paste into the Script tab. The word count and estimated runtime show
   immediately — if it is short, ask for a specific number of extra words on a
   named section rather than "make it longer".
3. Press *Split into scenes*, then check a few scene cards: the `image_prompt`
   under each one tells you what the app is about to search for. If several say
   "dark empty street at night", that passage was too abstract.
4. Render one chapter before committing to the full hour.
